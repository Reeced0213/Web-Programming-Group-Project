History Of Pac Man

The game Pac-Man originated in Japan towards the end of the 1970s, during a time when most arcade games were space shooters and combat-oriented. The game designer at Namco, Toru Iwatani, wanted to create something totally different, specifically an approachable, non-violent game that would appeal to more people, including women and families. With inspiration from ordinary things such as a pizza missing a slice, the Japanese word for “mouth,” and the onomatopoeic phrase “paku-paku,” Iwatani came up with the idea of a circular character, which he named Puck Man.

Pac-Man was largely unnoticed when it was released in Japan on May 22, 1980; however, Namco’s promotional efforts helped it gain popularity. Some of the unique features included in the game were: a maze with dots on a single screen, four differently designed ghosts that move in distinct ways, and power pellets that switch the roles of hunter and hunted. These aspects made the gameplay different from the shooting games, where only reflexes were required for success. The style and design of Pac-Man attracted more people to video gaming.

The US release of Pac-Man in October 1980 started a cultural phenomenon. After being renamed to protect arcade machines from vandalism, Pac-Man became extremely popular, selling more than 100,000 units in the US and earning billions of dollars. The character featured on music tracks, TV shows, clothing, and other products was one of the earliest true video game mascots. Pac-Man spawned many sequels and spinoffs, most notably Ms. Pac-Man, an updated version that introduced new mazes and improved ghost AI. But some Pac-Man versions were unsuccessful; Atari’s 1982 console version, released in haste, received widespread negative feedback and is considered a contributing factor in the 1983 video game crash.

Pac-Man’s legacy has continued to evolve. Pac-Man inspired franchises, the creation of high-score culture, and non-violent games. Its Level 256 kill screen was a challenge for gamers, who went on to set the first perfect score in 1999. Pac-Man today is a testament to what early gaming was all about, showing how an ingenious idea of an ever-hungry, yellow ball that moves through mazes revolutionized the entertainment industry and became one of the most iconic elements of video games.
