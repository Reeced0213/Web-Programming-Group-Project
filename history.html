History of Pac-Man

Origins and Design

  • Game designer Toru Iwatani created Pac-Man at Namco in the late 1970s.
  • He wanted an approachable, nonviolent game that could appeal to a broad audience, including women and families.
  • The character was inspired by a pizza missing a slice, the Japanese word for “mouth,” and “paku-paku,” an expression for eating. The original name was Puck Man.

First Release in Japan

  • Pac-Man was released in Japan on May 22, 1980.
  • Its single-screen maze, four ghosts with distinct movement patterns, and power pellets made it different from the space shooters popular at the time.
  • The approachable style helped bring more people into video gaming.

U.S. Breakthrough

  • The U.S. release in October 1980 sparked a cultural phenomenon.
  • The game became a major arcade hit and one of the earliest video game mascots to appear in music, television, clothing, and other products.
  • Its popularity led to many sequels and spin-offs, including Ms. Pac-Man, which added new mazes and updated ghost behavior.

Legacy and Challenges

  • Pac-Man helped establish high-score culture and inspired generations of nonviolent games.
  • The rushed Atari console version released in 1982 received widespread criticism.
  • The Level 256 “kill screen” became a challenge for players. The first perfect score was achieved in 1999.
